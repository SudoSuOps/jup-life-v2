Pulpboard: the absorbent, disposable default

Pulpboard is what most bars hand out. Coaster Factory says "pulpboard coasters provide excellent moisture absorption, soaking up drips and keeping tables dry," and that it "is intended for single or limited use, making it best for promotions or events" (Coaster Factory). It is also the cheapest way to get a logo on the table: businesses on a budget pick it "because it delivers professional branding at the lowest cost."

The tradeoff is waste. Washington Beer Blog notes that in the longer term pulpboard "could create more waste than cork since it is generally single-use" (Washington Beer Blog, June 2026).

Cork: reusable, and the sources disagree on water

Everyone agrees cork lasts longer than paper: "Cork coasters are reusable and maintain their look over multiple uses" (Coaster Factory). Whether it absorbs is another matter. Coaster Factory says cork has "a natural ability to absorb moisture and prevent water rings." Washington Beer Blog says the opposite: "Cork coasters will not absorb water; pulpboard coasters will absorb water." If absorbency matters to you, ask for a sample and test it under a cold glass.

Rubber: the most durable, doesn't absorb

Coaster Factory calls rubber "the most durable of all, capable of lasting for years with minimal wear," and notes rubber coasters "do not absorb liquid but protect surfaces through raised edges and non-slip texture." The catch is price: "rubber requires a higher upfront investment."

Which one should you pick?

  • A one-night event or festival: pulpboard. It's cheap and absorbent, and nobody needs it tomorrow.
  • Daily bar or restaurant service, where you'd rather reuse than reorder: a reusable coaster. Pick cork, rubber or printed coasters.
  • Favors guests take home, like at a wedding or event: something worth keeping. That's a reusable coaster too.
